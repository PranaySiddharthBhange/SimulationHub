Explicit evidence from the Stage 1 notes describes a quasi-static linear magnetic-circuit benchmark, not a valve or tank process. The physical structure is a rectangular iron core / iron core / core with square cross section side a = 25 mm = 0.025 m and cross-sectional area A = 0.000625 m^2. The core magnetic circuit includes a left leg, upper yoke, right leg iron section, lower yoke, and one discrete useful air gap. The air gap is named Air Gap, GAP-1, Gap, or air-gap across notes. A leakage branch/path is included in parallel with the useful air-gap path; this branch is named leakage-flux path, Leakage Branch, LEAK-1, leakage, or Leak-1. The branch is introduced after the right-leg iron and the two branches rejoin before the lower yoke, per 05_04_model_definition_notes.txt page 4. One note also represents the magnetic topology with CORE-U, CORE-R, GAP-1, LEAK-1, CORE-D, and MagneticGround, showing the series core path feeding a branch node, the useful branch through GAP-1 and leakage branch through LEAK-1, both rejoining into CORE-D, then terminating at magnetic ground; this supports the same branch topology but does not fully map CORE-U/CORE-R/CORE-D to the named left leg / yokes / right leg segments.

The electrical and sensing elements are an exciting coil / Exciting Coil / ExcitingCoil with 600 turns, a measuring coil / Measuring Coil / MeasuringCoil located in the air-gap region with 50 turns in the released configuration, and a useful-flux sensor / Flux Sensor / FluxSensor that measures or exposes useful air-gap flux. The exciting coil provides the magnetic potential source N*I and a positive RMS current-phasor magnitude through the exciting coil produces a positive magnetomotive force N*I. Positive exciting-coil current therefore creates positive magnetic mmf, and the resulting total core flux follows the defined positive magnetic direction. The measuring coil is magnetically coupled to the air-gap useful flux and its induced voltage orientation is negative relative to the useful air-gap flux direction / positive air-gap flux arrow. The exciting-coil induced voltage is oriented positive with respect to the defined positive electric direction. The measuring coil uses the opposite electric orientation to the exciting coil. The flux sensor sign is positive with the air-gap flux arrow.

Grounding is required in both domains. An electric ground / Electric Ground / ElectricGround defines zero electric potential and shall be present. A magnetic ground / Magnetic Ground / MagneticGround defines zero magnetic scalar potential and shall be present. The notes explicitly warn that omitting one of the grounds can leave the corresponding potential reference undefined.

Material and leakage parameters are revision-sensitive in the notes. Legacy values include mu_r = 1000, sigma = 0.05, and measuring coil turns = 40. Released values include effective relative permeability mu_r = 1200 under the linear benchmark assumption, leakage coefficient sigma = 0.08 for the released benchmark configuration, and measuring coil turns = 50. The notes do not explicitly provide an approval document id tying those dates to CR-MAG-004 or CR-MAG-006, so those approval-document entities are retained only as context and not used to resolve values.

The model kind is a quasi-static phasor magnetic-circuit model with linear reluctance elements. Magnetic flux, flux density, field strength, and magnetic potential are represented as quasi-static phasor quantities. For linear reluctance elements, reluctance remains real-valued and magnetic potential difference and flux have the same phasor angle. The baseline benchmark does not use a nonlinear B-H curve, hysteresis, or frequency-dependent permeability. Frequency is 50 Hz. The excitation input is not a command sequence but a prescribed RMS current-phasor magnitude schedule applied to the exciting coil: 0 A until 0.10 s, linear ramp from 0 A to 2.0 A RMS between 0.10 s and 0.50 s, then hold at 2.0 A through 1.0 s. Simulation stop / experiment duration is 1.0 s. Several notes repeat this schedule. In 12_09_analytic_verification_procedure.txt the scheduled-command entries stating 2.0 A RMS at t=0.10 s, 0.50 s, and 1.0 s conflict with the accompanying textual ramp definition; because the textual definition is repeated consistently across multiple notes and the tabulated command entries do not preserve the ramp shape, the benchmark schedule is resolved from the repeated textual ramp requirement, while the conflicting scheduled-command rows remain unresolved documentation artifacts.

The governing magnetic relationships explicitly stated in the notes are: B = Phi/A for constant cross-section elements; H_core = B/(mu0*mu_r) in iron; H_air = B/mu0 in air; magnetic potential difference of each element equals H times its mean flux-line length; reluctance for each linear flux-tube element is Rm = length/(mu0*mu_r*A) in iron and, by REQ-MAG-003 together with REQ-MAG-001 applied to air where mu_r = 1, the air-gap reluctance is R_gap = delta/(mu0*A). The theory note also states Phi = integral_A B_n dA, Vm = integral_l H_n dl, and Rm = Vm/Phi. Series-plus-parallel balance is explicit: the sum of series core magnetic potential drops plus the air-gap branch magnetic potential equals N*I; GAP-1 and LEAK-1 have equal magnetic potential difference because they are connected in parallel; flux continuity at the split is Phi_core = Phi_gap + Phi_leak. Useful air-gap flux is modeled as (1-sigma) times total core flux, so Phi_gap = (1-sigma)*Phi_core and Phi_leak = sigma*Phi_core. The leakage-reluctance coefficient form R_leak = R_gap*(1-sigma)/sigma is explicitly stated. The equivalent parallel reluctance is R_par = 1/(1/R_gap + 1/R_leak), and total reluctance is R_total = R_core + R_par. Derived from explicit equations and topology: Phi_core = N*I/R_total for the linear released benchmark. Units are consistent: reluctance in A/Wb, mmf / magnetic potential in A-turn, flux in Wb, B in T, H in A/m, induced voltage in V RMS.
